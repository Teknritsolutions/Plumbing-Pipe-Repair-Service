The small signs your pipes need attention.

IN THIS GUIDE

Notice the changes Make a useful note Respond safely Prepare for a visit

Notice the changes, however small.

Plumbing problems do not always begin with a dramatic leak. A damp patch beneath a sink, a recurring drip or a stain near a fitting may be a reason to ask for an assessment. These signs do not establish a diagnosis by themselves.

Pay attention to changes in familiar areas. Check visible connections without moving equipment or opening concealed spaces. Avoid touching hot pipes or anything close to electrical equipment.

Make a useful note.

Record where you noticed the issue, when it began and whether it changes when a fixture is used. A clear description can make an initial discussion more productive.

  • Where is the visible moisture?
  • Is it continuous or occasional?
  • Have you noticed a change in flow or drainage?
  • Can the area be accessed safely?

Respond safely.

If safe, use a known water shutoff to limit a significant leak. Keep away from wet electrical equipment and contaminated water. Do not attempt an unfamiliar repair or enter an unsafe area.

For immediate danger, contact the appropriate local emergency service. A website inquiry is not an emergency response channel.

Prepare for a professional visit.

Share your observations and discuss access, availability and assessment fees. Ask what the proposed work includes and how changes to the scope will be handled.

After a repair, request a clear explanation of the checks performed and any recommended follow-up. Keep the service record with your property maintenance notes.
